# Reading Rubric

When generating analytical notes, answer these questions where possible:

1. What concrete problem is the paper solving?
2. What is the core method or technical idea?
3. What data, benchmarks, or scenarios are used?
4. What evidence supports the claimed contribution?
5. What assumptions or limitations remain?
6. How is this paper relevant to the user's research agenda?
7. What should be reread, replicated, cited, or compared next?

When a research profile is available, also evaluate relation to the user's topic on a simple scale:

- 高: directly useful to the current topic, methods, or evidence base
- 中: adjacent and useful for framing, comparison, or methods
- 低: weakly related, mostly background or peripheral inspiration

Frontmatter discipline:

- Do not copy-paste the abstract into `theme`, `study_area`, `data_source`, `methodology`, `core_variable`, `key_finding`, or `relevance`.
- These fields must be extracted and compressed after understanding the paper.
- Use `references/frontmatter-rules.md` as the hard constraint for these fields.

For synthesis mode, identify recurring:

- task framing
- method families
- data sources
- strengths
- bottlenecks
- open problems
